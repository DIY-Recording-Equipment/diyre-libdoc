The MN-50's Colour boost is intentionally hot, and some of the variation you'll notice between two units is a normal side effect of how the module is sourced rather than a fault. The sections below are ordered by likelihood, so work down from the top.

## General resources

- [MN-50 Assembly Guide](http://www.diyrecordingequipment.com/pages/mn-50-manual)
- [General Troubleshooting Tips](/docs/troubleshooting/general/general-troubleshooting-tips/)

## 1. A pair of MN-50 modules don't match, with an audible gain difference between them

The MN-50's JFETs, trimpots, and ICs come preassembled from Avenson, the module's designer, rather than being hand-built by DIYRE, so component matching between two separately purchased units can vary more than with other Colour modules. If you need a closely matched stereo pair, ask for hand-picked matching when you place the order rather than assuming any two units will match automatically.

## 2. Output gain jumps dramatically, especially when chaining modules

The MN-50's Colour boost runs hot by design, with a gain increase around 20dB. If that makes it unwieldy when chaining multiple modules together, that's a characteristic of the module's design rather than something adjustable on the DIYRE side, since the MN-50's circuit is licensed from Avenson rather than a DIYRE design.

## 3. Unit purchased as a bare PCB from a third party isn't passing audio

DIYRE can only support MN-50 kits purchased directly, not third-party PCB-only versions. If you're working from a bare board obtained elsewhere, standard cold-joint and component-orientation checks still apply, but documentation for that specific board may differ from the DIYRE kit guide.
